Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Riverside, CA

Home / Blog / Signs Your Air Conditioner Is Failing in Riverside, CA

As the peak of summer approach here in Riverside, CA, our air conditioners become essential appliances of our home comfort. They work tirelessly to keep our indoor spaces cool and comfortable. But like any mechanical system, air conditioners will eventually wear down and will eventually begin to show signs of wear and tear, indicating that a significant issue or breakdown might be on the horizon. Being aware of these symptoms can save you from a hot and inconvenient situation on the hottest day of the year, and potentially avoid more extensive problems in the long run. Here at Riverside HVAC, we want to help you understand what to look out for.

Reduced Cooling Performance: A Lack of Consistent Cooling?

One of the most obvious and frustrating signs that your air conditioner is struggling is a drop in its cooling capability. You may constantly be tweaking the thermostat down only to still feel warm or humid indoors. Uneven cooling throughout your house is a tell-tale sign. This isn't just about comfort; it indicates a problem within the system. Several factors could contribute to this issue. It could be a refrigerant leak, which means your system isn't able to absorb and release heat effectively. The refrigerant is what makes cooling possible. Another common culprit is a obstructed air filter, which restricts airflow and makes the unit work harder without achieving the desired cooling. Evaporator coils caked with grime can hinder performance, as they cannot efficiently transfer heat. A struggling compressor, the core of the cooling cycle, might be the problem. If you're experiencing inadequate cooling, it's time to have a professional from Riverside HVAC diagnose the problem.

Strange Noises: When Your AC Sounds Off

Your air conditioner should operate with a relatively consistent, low hum. If you start hearing unexpected auditory disturbances coming from your unit, it's a strong signal that something is amiss. Worn motor bearings are often the source of grinding or squealing sounds. These bearings, key to rotational movement, can wear down over time, leading to friction and eventual motor failure if not addressed. Electrical issues, like a failing relay, can manifest as constant clicking. If you hear banging or clunking, be very concerned, as they might suggest a major mechanical failure within the compressor or fan. Ignoring these noises can lead to more extensive damage. Heeding these sound warnings can prevent minor issues from becoming major expenses.

Increased Energy Bills: An Unexpected Surge in Utility Costs

Your electric bill mysteriously climbing is a common indicator of AC troubles. When your energy consumption hasn't changed but your bills are spiking, your AC unit might be working harder than it should to achieve the same level of cooling. This inefficiency can be caused by a variety of factors. As mentioned, a clogged air filter reduces airflow, forcing the fan motor to work overtime. If your ducts are leaking, you're cooling your attic instead of your home, meaning your AC has to run longer to maintain the desired temperature. An old or undersized unit might just be inefficient by design. Refrigerant leaks also force the system to work harder. Over time, components like the compressor and fan motors can lose their efficiency. When your energy bills are unexpectedly high, it's worth having Riverside HVAC inspect your AC unit to identify the source of the inefficiency. We can help you understand if a repair or a more efficient replacement makes sense.

Poor Airflow: Weak Air from Your Vents?

Proper airflow ensures consistent cooling and even temperatures across all rooms. If you notice that the air coming out of your vents is weak, or if some rooms have a mere whisper of air than others, it's a strong indication of an underlying problem. Poor airflow can be attributed to multiple factors. A very common culprit is a dirty or clogged air filter, which restricts the volume of air that can pass through your system. Obstructed return vents can impede the system's ability to pull air in. Problems with the fan motor, separate from the filter, can also cause weak air delivery. A diminished fan motor means less air reaching your vents. Ductwork blockages or damage can severely limit airflow. Closed dampers in your duct system can lead to inconsistent airflow distribution. Fixing poor airflow is vital not only for your comfort but also to prevent overworking your AC and reducing its longevity.

Excessive Moisture or Leaks: Puddles Around Your AC Unit?

A little condensation is fine, but if you see puddles or a lot of wetness, there's an issue. This is a clear red flag that something is wrong. The most common cause of water leaks is a clogged condensate drain line. As your AC cools the air, it removes humidity, and this moisture collects in a drain pan and flows out through a condensate line. If this line becomes blocked by algae, dirt, or debris, the water will back up and overflow the pan. This can lead to water damage to your home, and in some cases, even electrical hazards. Leaks can also originate from a cracked or rusted drain pan. In more severe cases, a refrigerant leak can also cause ice to form on the evaporator coils, which then melts and overflows the drain pan. Any standing water or excessive moisture near your AC, it's imperative to address it quickly to prevent further damage. Riverside HVAC can diagnose the source of the leak and provide a lasting solution.

Frequent Cycling: Is Your AC Turning On and Off Too Often?

Normal AC operation involves consistent cycles of cooling and then resting. If you notice your AC unit turning on and off much more frequently than usual, this is known as "short cycling" and it's a significant warning sign. When an AC short cycles, it fails to finish a complete cooling cycle, leading to inefficiency and system strain. This can be caused by several issues. An improperly sized unit (too large for your home) can sometimes short cycle because it cools the space too quickly and then shuts off before it can properly dehumidify. However, more often, short cycling points to a problem like a low refrigerant charge, a dirty air filter, or even a faulty thermostat. A struggling compressor or an electrical fault can also be culprits. Regardless of the cause, frequent short cycling wears down your AC components much faster, leading to premature breakdowns and increased energy consumption. An AC that short cycles isn't working properly and requires professional diagnosis.

Old Age: Considering the Age of Your System

Even if your air conditioner isn't exhibiting any dramatic symptoms, its age is a crucial factor in predicting its eventual failure. A central AC system generally has a lifespan of 10 to 15 years, though some last longer with diligent care. As your unit approaches or exceeds this age range, it becomes more prone to breakdowns and less efficient. Older units often use outdated refrigerants like R-22, which are no longer manufactured and are becoming increasingly expensive to service due to supply limitations. Years of operation mean components in older systems are simply more fatigued. Though maintenance helps, eventually repairs become too frequent and expensive compared to replacing with a new unit. When your AC is over 10 years old and exhibiting other warning signs, it's time to think about replacement. Investing in a new, energy-efficient AC can lead to considerable savings and improved comfort for the long term.
