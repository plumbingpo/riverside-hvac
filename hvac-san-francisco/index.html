When Your System Fails in San Francisco

Heating and cooling failures rarely wait for a convenient day, which is why we offer prompt, flexible scheduling for most situations. When you call, you'll reach a real person who will get a technician headed to your door quickly. Arrival in the area is usually within a day for urgent calls, and a dead AC in a heat wave or a furnace out on a freezing night is the kind of call we move to the front of the line.

The emergency calls we run usually follow a pattern. On the cooling side, it's warm air from the vents, an outdoor unit that hums without starting, or an evaporator coil frozen solid. On the heating side, it's a furnace that lights and then shuts down. Year-round, it's breakers that trip whenever the system starts, clogged condensate drains backing water into the furnace cabinet, and thermostats that have quit. Because our trucks carry capacitors, contactors, igniters, flame sensors, universal control boards, and fittings, the majority of these calls get fixed in one visit, with the price agreed before any work begins.

A little caution while you wait for us can prevent a much larger repair. A frozen air conditioner should be shut off at the thermostat with the fan set to "on", which lets the coil thaw safely. Running it frozen can flood the drain pan and damage the compressor. A furnace that keeps short-cycling, or any burning smell, means it's time to shut the unit off at its service switch and call us right away.

Keeping Your San Francisco HVAC System Running Longer

A little routine care makes all the difference between a system that runs for years and one that quits in the middle of a heat wave. Our advice for homeowners is a short list: fresh filters every one to three months, two feet of clearance around the outdoor condenser, a gentle rinse each spring for the condenser coil, and two professional tune-ups a year, so problems are found while they're still inexpensive.

Age matters too. Most central air conditioners run 12 to 15 years, furnaces 15 to 20, and heat pumps 10 to 15 with regular service. If your system is past that window and starting to struggle, such as rising energy bills, rooms that never feel comfortable, or a service call every season, a planned replacement is nearly always less expensive and much easier on you than an emergency one during the first heat wave of summer.

Small problems don't stay minor when they're ignored. Consider a weak capacitor: replacing it is a quick, affordable repair, but if it's ignored for a season, it can burn out the compressor, and just like that a minor fix has become a full system replacement. That's why we encourage homeowners to keep our number in their phone and reach out at the first sign of trouble. Catching it early saves time and money and prevents a miserable night.
